1. Transcription ▼
O a veces le cae a uno encima. Y ahí es fractura… asegurada.
2. Phonetics ▼
Oh ah beh-ses | leh kah-eh | ah oo-no | en-see-mah. | Yah-ee es | frahk-too-rah… | ah-seh-goo-rah-dah.
3. Translation ▼
Or sometimes it falls right on top of you. At that point it’s a fracture… guaranteed.
4. Contextual Explanation ▼
The line is a warning: if something heavy drops on a person, the result is basically a guaranteed broken bone. The pause before asegurada adds emphasis.
5. Contextual Vocab ▼
a veces — sometimes
le cae — it falls on (someone)
a uno — on one / on you
encima — on top
fractura — fracture
asegurada — guaranteed
6. Grammar ▼
Spanish often describes accidents impersonally. Instead of saying "the ladder fell on me", Spanish says "le cae a uno encima" meaning "it falls on someone".
