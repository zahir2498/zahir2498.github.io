1. Transcription ▼
O a veces le cae a uno encima. Y ahí es fractura… asegurada.
2. Phonetics ▼
Oh ah beh-ses | leh kah-eh | ah oo-no | en-see-mah. | Yah-ee es | frahk-too-rah… | ah-seh-goo-rah-dah.
3. Translation ▼
Or sometimes it falls right on top of you. At that point it’s a fracture… guaranteed.
4. Contextual Explanation ▼
The line is a warning: if something heavy drops on a person, the result is basically a guaranteed broken bone. The pause before asegurada adds emphasis, like “…yup, for sure.”
5. Contextual Vocab ▼
a veces — sometimes
le cae — it falls on (someone)
a uno — on one / on you (general)
encima — on top (of)
ahí — there/then → here: “that’s when”
fractura — fracture
asegurada — guaranteed
6. Grammar ▼
This line shows how Spanish often talks about accidents in an impersonal way. Instead of saying “the ladder fell on me,” the speaker says “le cae a uno encima” — literally “it falls on one.” The word le marks the person affected, while a uno makes it general (“on you / on a person”). Y ahí works as a connector meaning “and that’s when,” setting up the result: es fractura asegurada = “it’s a guaranteed fracture.” Notice they drop una before fractura — this makes it punchier and faster in conversation.

Verb tense:
cae is the present tense of caer (“to fall”), third person singular — “it falls.” It’s used here to describe what can happen generally.
